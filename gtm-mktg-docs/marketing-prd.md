# Marketing PRD

## Overview

A Marketing PRD is a detailed requirements document for marketing work. It defines the outcome you want, the audience you serve, the deliverable you are shipping, and how success will be measured — in enough depth to direct execution.

**The PRD is the second step, not the first.** Before writing the PRD, write a [Marketing Spec](marketing-spec.md). The spec defines the problem and aligns stakeholders on the bet. The PRD translates that alignment into detailed requirements. The brief then turns those requirements into execution direction.

**Spec → PRD → Brief**

This sequence is useful with or without AI, but it becomes especially valuable when AI is involved. Large language models are non-deterministic, so broad requests tend to produce broad, averaged-down output. A well-defined spec fed into a detailed PRD creates tighter plans, more relevant output, and significantly less back-and-forth refinement.

## Why use a Marketing PRD?

Marketing teams often ship campaigns, content, launches, and other deliverables without first defining the steering logic behind them. That usually leads to:

- More output, but not necessarily more impact
- Extra revisions caused by unclear direction
- Work that is hard to evaluate after launch
- AI-generated plans that sound complete but are not grounded in the right inputs

A Marketing PRD helps prevent that by making the core requirements explicit up front.

## Core mindset

Marketers may support the sale of a product or service, but marketing also has products of its own: campaigns, launches, briefs, assets, and programs.

If those deliverables are products, they benefit from the same kind of structured thinking used in product and engineering work:

- clear requirements
- defined scope
- audience understanding
- quality judgment
- measurable success criteria

The goal is not to add bureaucracy. The goal is to create enough structure to steer execution well.

## The five questions a Marketing PRD must answer

### 1. Purpose

What outcome are we driving, and what problem are we solving?

This section should make the intended result explicit. It should explain why the work exists and what change the team wants to create.

Helpful prompts:

- What are we trying to make happen?
- What problem or friction are we addressing?
- Why does this matter now?

### 2. Impact

What is the scope of impact, and why is this worth doing?

This is the bet behind the work. It helps teams distinguish meaningful opportunities from interesting but low-value tasks.

Helpful prompts:

- What happens if this works?
- Who or what is affected?
- Why are we willing to invest in this now?

### 3. Audience

Who is this for, what do they want, and what are they trying to get done?

This is more than naming a segment. It should capture the audience's motivations, desired progress, and relevant pain points. Strong audience understanding makes it easier to judge whether a concept, message, or deliverable is actually useful.

Helpful prompts:

- Who is the intended audience?
- What do they want to do, feel, or become?
- What is getting in their way today?
- What signals would tell us we understand them well enough?

### 4. Deliverable

What exactly are we shipping, and what are we deliberately not shipping?

This section defines the output and keeps scope from drifting. It should describe the deliverable clearly enough that a teammate, partner, or AI system can support execution without inventing major assumptions.

Helpful prompts:

- What are we producing?
- What format, channel, or package is included?
- What is explicitly out of scope?
- What quality bar must the deliverable meet?

### 5. Measurement

How will we measure success, and when will we evaluate it?

Success criteria should be specific enough to inform decisions after launch. If possible, define a SMART goal: specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound.

Helpful prompts:

- What is the primary success metric?
- What secondary signals matter?
- What baseline are we comparing against?
- When will we review performance and decide what to do next?

## Using a Marketing PRD with AI

AI does not replace judgment. It compresses execution and increases the importance of steering.

Use the Marketing PRD as structured input when working with AI:

- Provide the PRD before asking for plans or drafts
- Include relevant context documents, constraints, and brand rules
- Ask the model to identify gaps or ambiguities before generating outputs
- Review for drift, hallucinations, and low-value filler
- Keep humans responsible for prioritization, audience understanding, and quality judgment

Good AI use is not about delegating the whole role. It is about giving the tool enough structured knowledge to help with execution while a human remains accountable for direction and discernment.

## Recommended workflow

1. Write the [Marketing Spec](marketing-spec.md) — define the problem, audience, and bet before writing requirements
2. Use the spec as the foundation for this PRD
3. Validate the audience understanding and success criteria
4. Use the PRD to brief teammates, partners, or AI systems
5. Produce deliverables such as campaigns, launch plans, or content using the [Campaign Brief Template](templates/campaign-brief-template.md)
6. Measure results against the stated success criteria
7. Refine the spec and PRD pattern based on what you learn

## Common pitfalls

- Starting with tactics before defining the outcome
- Confusing audience labels with real audience understanding
- Leaving scope ambiguous
- Asking AI for output without enough context
- Measuring activity instead of impact
- Treating more output as proof of better work
