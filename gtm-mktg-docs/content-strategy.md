# Content Strategy

## Overview

Content strategy defines what content you create, for whom, and why. It ensures that every piece of content serves a clear purpose and contributes to your marketing goals. This document covers frameworks for developing and executing a content strategy that supports your go-to-market objectives.

## What is Content Strategy?

Content strategy is the planning, development, and management of content to achieve specific business and marketing goals. It answers:
- **Who** are you creating content for?
- **What** topics and formats will you focus on?
- **Why** does each piece of content exist?
- **Where** will content be published and distributed?
- **How** will you measure content effectiveness?

A content strategy connects your [positioning and messaging](positioning-messaging.md) to the content your audience actually finds and consumes.

## Why Content Strategy Matters

Without a content strategy, content becomes:
- **Inconsistent**: Different voices, messages, and formats
- **Reactive**: Created when someone thinks of an idea, not when the audience needs it
- **Unmeasured**: No way to know what's working
- **Disconnected**: Doesn't support broader marketing goals

A strong content strategy ensures your content compounds over time, builds trust with your audience, and drives measurable results.

## Content Strategy Framework

### Step 1: Define Content Goals

Align your content goals with your broader marketing objectives:

**Awareness Goals:**
- Help new audiences discover you
- Establish credibility and expertise in your category
- Build brand recognition

**Consideration Goals:**
- Educate prospects on how you solve their problems
- Help prospects evaluate whether you're a good fit
- Address common objections and questions

**Conversion Goals:**
- Move prospects toward taking action (trial, purchase, signup)
- Provide the information needed to make a decision
- Reduce friction in the buying process

**Retention Goals:**
- Help existing customers get more value from your product
- Build community and loyalty
- Encourage expansion and referral

**Connect goals to metrics:**
- What specific, measurable outcome does each content goal drive?
- How will you track whether content is achieving its goal?
- See [Analytics Strategy](../measurement-docs/analytics-strategy.md) for measurement frameworks

### Step 2: Know Your Audience

Content is only effective if it's relevant to the people consuming it:

**Map content to personas:**
- Which [buyer personas](buyer-personas.md) are you creating content for?
- What topics and questions matter most to each persona?
- What format preferences does each persona have?

**Understand the content journey:**
- What content does your audience seek at each stage of their journey?
- What questions do they have before they know about your product?
- What information do they need to make a decision?

**Research audience needs:**
- What are they searching for online?
- What questions do they ask in communities and forums?
- What content do they share and engage with?
- Use [user research methods](../product-docs/user-research-discovery.md) to gather insights

### Step 3: Define Your Content Pillars

Content pillars are the core topics your content strategy focuses on. They should:
- Align with your product's positioning and value proposition
- Address your audience's key needs and questions
- Reflect your brand's areas of expertise
- Be broad enough to generate many pieces of content over time

**Typically 3-5 pillars:**
- Pillar 1: [Core topic that connects to your primary differentiator]
- Pillar 2: [Topic that addresses your audience's biggest challenge]
- Pillar 3: [Topic that positions you as an authority in your category]

**Examples:**
- A productivity tool might have pillars: Time Management, Focus & Deep Work, Team Collaboration
- A developer tool might have pillars: Code Quality, Engineering Efficiency, Team Workflows

### Step 4: Plan Content Formats and Channels

Different formats work better for different purposes and audiences:

**Long-form Content:**
- Blog posts, guides, and articles
- Establish expertise and drive organic search traffic
- Best for educational content that requires depth

**Short-form Content:**
- Social media posts, short videos, newsletter content
- Build awareness and drive frequent touchpoints
- Best for insights, tips, and timely content

**Visual Content:**
- Infographics, charts, diagrams
- Make complex ideas accessible
- Best for data, frameworks, and step-by-step guides

**Video Content:**
- Product demos, tutorials, interviews, behind-the-scenes
- Build trust and demonstrate value
- Best for showing how things work, telling stories

**Interactive Content:**
- Templates, calculators, assessments, tools
- Deliver immediate value and generate leads
- Best for helping audience solve a specific problem

**Match format to channel:**
- What formats work best on each channel?
- See [Channel Strategy](channel-strategy.md) for channel selection
- Match content depth to where your audience is in their journey

### Step 5: Develop Your Editorial Calendar

An editorial calendar makes your content strategy operational:

**Planning cadence:**
- Plan themes and major content quarterly
- Plan specific pieces monthly
- Execute and optimize weekly

**What to include:**
- Content topic and format
- Target audience (which persona)
- Content goal (awareness, consideration, conversion, retention)
- Publication channel
- Publication date
- Owner/author
- Status

**Content cadence:**
- How often will you publish on each channel?
- Can you sustain your planned cadence with available resources?
- Better to publish less frequently with higher quality than frequently with lower quality

### Step 6: Create a Content Production Process

Consistent quality requires a repeatable process:

**Content Creation:**
- Use [Content Brief Template](templates/content-brief-template.md) to plan each piece
- Define research, writing, review, and approval steps
- Establish quality standards (accuracy, clarity, tone)

**Review and Approval:**
- Who reviews content before publication?
- What does the review process check for?
- How are feedback and revisions managed?

**Publication and Distribution:**
- Who publishes content to each channel?
- What steps happen at publication (metadata, tags, formatting)?
- How is content promoted after publication?

**Repurposing:**
- How can content be adapted for different formats or channels?
- Which pieces are worth repurposing and when?
- Repurposing extends the value of your best content

### Step 7: Measure Content Performance

Content measurement should connect to content goals:

**Awareness Metrics:**
- Organic traffic and reach
- Social shares and impressions
- Brand search volume

**Engagement Metrics:**
- Time on page, scroll depth
- Comments, reactions, shares
- Email open and click rates

**Conversion Metrics:**
- Leads generated from content
- Trials or signups attributed to content
- Revenue influenced by content

**Retention Metrics:**
- Return visit rate
- Newsletter subscriber retention
- Community growth and engagement

**Review and optimize:**
- Review content performance monthly
- Double down on what's working
- Sunset or update underperforming content
- Use [analytics strategy](../measurement-docs/analytics-strategy.md) for measurement

## Content Quality Standards

Good content should be:

**Accurate:**
- Factually correct and up to date
- Includes citations or evidence where appropriate
- Reviewed by subject matter experts

**Useful:**
- Provides genuine value to the reader
- Answers real questions your audience has
- Actionable where possible

**Clear:**
- Easy to understand without jargon
- Well-organized with logical flow
- Appropriate reading level for your audience

**On-brand:**
- Consistent tone and voice
- Aligned with your positioning and messaging
- Reflects your brand's values and personality

**Discoverable:**
- Optimized for search where relevant
- Well-titled and described for sharing
- Tagged and categorized appropriately

## Content Strategy Best Practices

- **Start with Your Audience**: Create content for people, not for search engines or algorithms
- **Quality Over Quantity**: One excellent piece is worth more than ten mediocre ones
- **Be Consistent**: A predictable publication cadence builds audience habits
- **Think Long-term**: Content compounds over time; prioritize evergreen content
- **Repurpose Strategically**: Adapt high-performing content for different formats and channels
- **Measure What Matters**: Track metrics that connect to real business outcomes
- **Maintain a Single Voice**: Consistency in tone and messaging builds trust

## Common Pitfalls

**Creating Content Without Goals:**
- Publishing content without knowing what it's supposed to achieve
- Results in unfocused content that doesn't drive results
- Solution: Define the goal and target persona before creating any piece

**Prioritizing Production Over Quality:**
- Publishing too frequently with low-quality content
- Damages credibility and audience trust
- Solution: Set a sustainable cadence that allows quality work

**Ignoring Distribution:**
- Creating great content but not promoting it
- Even excellent content needs active distribution
- Solution: Spend as much effort on distribution as creation

**Not Updating Old Content:**
- Letting older content become outdated and inaccurate
- Old content still ranks and is found by new readers
- Solution: Schedule regular content audits and updates

**Skipping Measurement:**
- Not tracking what content is performing and why
- Can't improve without knowing what works
- Solution: Define success metrics before publishing
